Working Papers

Second-Year Paper
Innovation Dynamics and Acquisition Decisions
Working Paper
Abstract
Technological similarity helps determine a target’s value to an acquirer, but it changes as both firms innovate. I show that the history of this relationship helps explain an acquirer’s choice among observably similar targets.

Using semantic embeddings of firms’ patent portfolios, I measure technological distance, with smaller distances indicating greater similarity. I summarize this history using technological convergence speed, which captures how quickly distance changes over time.

To address endogeneity from strategic patenting, I use patent-examiner leniency as an instrument. The IV estimates imply that a one-standard-deviation increase in distance raises acquisition probability by 2.1 percentage points. Conditional on current distance, a one-standard-deviation increase in convergence speed lowers it by 3.4 percentage points.
Paper
Scheduled for presentation at the Financial Management Association Annual Meeting (FMA), 2026 and the Southern Finance Association Annual Meeting (SFA), 2026.
